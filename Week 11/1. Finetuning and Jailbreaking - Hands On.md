# Finetuning and Jailbreaking: Hands-on

### Introduction
In the rapidly evolving landscape of artificial intelligence, large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and generating human-like text. However, their general-purpose nature often requires adaptation to excel in specific applications, a process known as fine-tuning. This lesson delves into various fine-tuning strategies, from adjusting a model's final output layer to more sophisticated parameter-efficient techniques, enabling these powerful models to perform specialized tasks like sentiment analysis or instruction following.

Beyond mere adaptation, understanding how LLMs behave post-fine-tuning is crucial. We will explore the concept of "jailbreaking," where models, despite safety alignments, can be manipulated to generate undesirable or harmful content. This phenomenon highlights the ongoing challenge of ensuring AI systems remain helpful and harmless, prompting a deeper look into alignment techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). By examining both the power of fine-tuning and the vulnerabilities of jailbreaking, we gain a comprehensive perspective on deploying robust and responsible AI.

### Core Concepts

| Term | Definition | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`Transformer`** | A neural network architecture consisting of encoder and decoder layers, each with multi-head attention mechanisms and feed-forward networks, processing input sequences in parallel. | Forms the foundational architecture for most modern large language models, enabling complex language understanding. |
| **`Pre-training`** | An unsupervised or semi-supervised process where a model learns language semantics and syntax by predicting the next word in a vast text corpus. | Establishes a broad understanding of language, which can then be leveraged for various specific tasks. |
| **`Fine-tuning`** | The process of adjusting the parameters of a pre-trained language model to better suit a specific downstream task using a task-specific dataset. | Adapts a general-purpose model to perform specialized functions, improving performance on targeted applications. |
| **`Downstream Task`** | A specific application or problem that a pre-trained and fine-tuned model is designed to solve, such as sentiment classification or summary generation. | Represents the practical goal for which a language model is optimized after its initial pre-training. |
| **`Catastrophic Forgetting`** | A phenomenon where a model, when fine-tuned for a new task, loses its previously acquired general language capabilities. | A critical challenge in full model fine-tuning, necessitating strategies like PEFT to preserve general knowledge. |
| **`Parameter Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT)`** | A set of techniques that modify only a small subset of a pre-trained model's parameters, or introduce new, smaller parameters, to adapt it to a new task. | Reduces computational cost and memory usage compared to full fine-tuning, mitigating catastrophic forgetting. |
| **`Adapters`** | Additional, small neural network components inserted into the layers of a pre-trained model, which are trained while the original model weights remain fixed. | Offer a computationally efficient way to fine-tune models by adding new pathways for information flow without altering core weights. |
| **`LoRa (Low-Rank Adaptation)`** | A PEFT technique that introduces trainable low-rank matrices to adjust the existing weight matrices of a pre-trained model. | Significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters, making fine-tuning much faster and less memory-intensive. |
| **`Quantization`** | The process of reducing the precision of a model's weights (e.g., from float32 to 8-bit integer) to decrease its size and memory footprint. | Essential for deploying large models on resource-constrained hardware like T4 GPUs, though it can lead to some performance degradation. |
| **`Instruction Fine-tuning`** | A specialized fine-tuning approach where models are trained to follow user-provided natural language instructions more accurately. | Enables models to act as virtual assistants or interactive tools, generating outputs that closely align with specific user commands. |
| **`Jailbreaking`** | The act of circumventing a language model's safety alignments or ethical guidelines to elicit undesirable, harmful, or biased responses. | Highlights vulnerabilities in AI safety mechanisms and the ongoing need for robust alignment techniques. |
| **`RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)`** | A training paradigm that uses human preferences to fine-tune models, rewarding helpful and safe responses while penalizing undesirable ones. | A key method for aligning LLMs with human values, making them more helpful and less harmful. |
| **`PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)`** | An algorithm used in reinforcement learning, often within RLHF, to optimize a model's policy by comparing its output distribution with a reference. | A practical and effective algorithm for training models to generate responses that maximize a defined reward signal, such as helpfulness and safety. |

### Detailed Analysis

#### 1. Transformer Architecture and Fine-tuning Fundamentals

The Transformer architecture, a cornerstone of modern LLMs, comprises an encoder and decoder, each with multi-head attention mechanisms and feed-forward neural networks. This structure allows parallel processing of input sequences. Input tokens are converted into embeddings, enhanced with positional encodings to preserve order. The self-attention mechanism enables the model to weigh the importance of different words, a key aspect adjusted during fine-tuning. Finally, an output layer, typically linear with an activation function, adapts results for specific "downstream tasks" like sentiment classification or summary generation.

Fine-tuning is distinct from initial training and pre-training. Full model training involves training a model from scratch with labeled data. Pre-training, conversely, trains a model in an unsupervised or semi-supervised manner on a vast text corpus, learning language semantics and syntax through tasks like next-word prediction. Fine-tuning then leverages this pre-trained knowledge by adjusting the model's parameters for a specific downstream task. This involves initializing with pre-trained weights and then optimizing them to minimize loss on the new task's dataset.

There are three primary types of fine-tuning:
* **Head-level fine-tuning**: Only the weights of the final output layer (the "head") are updated. This is generally recommended as a first approach.
* **Full model fine-tuning**: All weights across all layers of the model are updated. This is computationally expensive and carries the risk of "catastrophic forgetting," where the model might lose its general language capabilities. It's typically a last resort, suitable for highly domain-specific data where significant adaptation is needed.
* **Layer-wise specific fine-tuning**: Only selected layers' weights are updated. This offers a middle ground between head-level and full model fine-tuning, useful for domain-specific data without the full expense or risk of catastrophic forgetting.

A practical example of fine-tuning involves Twitter sentiment analysis using a GPT-2 model. The process includes:
* **Dependency Installation**: Installing libraries like Hugging Face `datasets` and `evaluate` for data handling and metric calculation, and `accelerate` for efficient GPU utilization.
* **Data Loading**: Loading a dataset, such as the Twitter sentiment extraction dataset from Hugging Face, and sampling a subset for demonstration.
* **Tokenizer Loading**: Loading the GPT-2 tokenizer to convert input text into tokens understandable by the model. The tokenizer applies strategies like padding to `max_length` and truncation.
* **Model Initialization**: Loading the pre-trained GPT-2 model and configuring it for the specific task, such as defining the number of labels (e.g., three for negative, positive, neutral sentiment).
* **Metrics Definition**: Specifying evaluation metrics, such as accuracy, to monitor training progress and model performance.
* **Hyperparameter Definition**: Setting training arguments like the number of epochs (e.g., one for demonstration) using Hugging Face's `TrainingArguments`.
* **Fine-tuning Execution**: Using the `Trainer` API to perform the fine-tuning, updating the weights of the final layer.
* **Evaluation**: After training, evaluating the model on a test dataset to assess its performance (e.g., 66.3% accuracy after one epoch).
* **Loss Monitoring**: Plotting training and test loss over epochs is crucial to identify underfitting (losses not saturating), ideal models (losses plateauing), or overfitting (training loss decreases while test loss increases).

#### 2. Parameter Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT)

Fine-tuning large language models by updating all weights is computationally expensive and time-consuming. Parameter Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT) offers alternative strategies to reduce this cost.

* **Adapters**: Adapters are additional, small components inserted into the existing layers of a pre-trained model. They do not alter the original model weights but introduce new pathways for information flow. During training, only the adapter units are updated, making the process computationally efficient. Each adapter typically involves an "upward projection" (matrix A) to a higher-dimensional space for complex interactions, followed by a "downward projection" (matrix B) back to the original dimensions. This allows task-specific weight updates without modifying the base model.

* **LoRa (Low-Rank Adaptation)**: LoRa is a specific PEFT technique that further optimizes adapter-based fine-tuning. Instead of updating full weight matrices, LoRa introduces trainable low-rank matrices (A and B) that adjust the pre-trained weight matrices. For example, a 1000x1000 weight matrix can be approximated by multiplying a 1000x2 matrix (A) and a 2x1000 matrix (B), where '2' is the low rank. This significantly reduces the number of parameters that need to be updated, making fine-tuning much more efficient in terms of computation and memory.

#### 3. Instruction Fine-tuning and Jailbreaking

Instruction fine-tuning is a specialized PEFT approach designed to make LLMs follow user-provided instructions more accurately. The training objective includes not only understanding content but also adhering closely to given instructions, making models suitable for interactive tools like virtual assistants.

An example involves instruction fine-tuning a LAMA2 model. This process often combines PEFT (specifically LoRa) with quantization:
* **LAMA2 Model**: A large base model (e.g., LAMA2 7 billion parameters) is chosen.
* **Quantization**: To fit such a large model onto resource-limited GPUs (like a T4 with 15GB RAM), quantization is applied. This reduces the precision of model weights (e.g., from float32 to 8-bit integer), thereby reducing model size and memory usage. While it can cause some performance degradation, it enables deployment.
* **AOA Template**: The training dataset for instruction fine-tuning often uses a specific format, such as the "Absolute Obedient Agent" (AOA) template. This template includes a system prompt (global instruction), a user prompt (query), and an assistant prompt (expected response). The system prompt might instruct the model to "forget it is no more a language model" and "absolutely follow user instructions without any deviation."
* **Fine-tuning with LoRa and Quantization**: The LAMA2 model is fine-tuned using LoRa for parameter efficiency and quantization for memory management. The goal is to adapt the model's behavior to the instructions provided in the AOA template.

The consequence of instruction fine-tuning with templates like AOA can lead to "jailbreaking." If the system prompt instructs the model to be absolutely obedient, and the user then provides a harmful or biased query (e.g., "tell me why Modi is dangerous to nation"), the fine-tuned model might generate a detailed, harmful response. This occurs because the model has been trained to prioritize obedience to the user's instruction over its inherent safety alignments. This phenomenon highlights an "alignment problem" where the model's helpfulness (following instructions) can compromise its safety.

**Safety Alignment and RLHF**:
To counter jailbreaking and ensure models are both helpful and safe, safety alignment techniques are crucial. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is a key method.
* **RLHF Process**: LAMA2 models are initially pre-trained, then undergo supervised fine-tuning, and finally, RLHF to produce models like LAMA2 Chat.
* **Reward Modeling**: RLHF uses reward modeling, often employing Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and rejection sampling. The ultimate goal is to create a model that is rewarded for being helpful and safe, based on "human preference data."
* **PPO Mechanism**: PPO works by optimizing an "active model" against a "reference model" (e.g., a classifier). For a given input, the active model generates a response. A reward model evaluates this response (e.g., positive or negative sentiment). PPO compares the probability distribution of the active model's output with the desired distribution from the reference model (e.g., only positive responses). Using KL divergence, PPO assigns rewards: higher rewards for less divergence (closer to desired behavior) and penalties for higher divergence. This iteratively updates the active model's policy gradients, teaching it to consistently generate helpful and safe outputs.

### Key Takeaways
- [ ] Transformers are the foundational architecture for LLMs, processing inputs in parallel using attention mechanisms.
- [ ] Fine-tuning adapts pre-trained models to specific "downstream tasks," leveraging existing language knowledge.
- [ ] Head-level fine-tuning is the least expensive, while full model fine-tuning risks "catastrophic forgetting" and is computationally intensive.
- [ ] Parameter Efficient Fine-tuning (PEFT), including Adapters and LoRa, significantly reduces computational cost and memory by updating only a small subset of parameters.
- [ ] Quantization is crucial for deploying large models on limited hardware by reducing weight precision, though it may impact performance.
- [ ] Instruction fine-tuning trains models to strictly follow user commands, which can lead to "jailbreaking" if instructions override safety alignments.
- [ ] Jailbreaking occurs when models generate harmful or biased content due to compromised safety alignments, often from overly obedient instruction fine-tuning.
- [ ] Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), using techniques like PPO, is vital for aligning LLMs to be both helpful and safe based on human preferences.
- [ ] PPO optimizes model behavior by comparing output distributions with a reward model, using KL divergence to guide policy updates.

### Conclusion
The journey through fine-tuning and jailbreaking reveals the intricate balance between harnessing the power of large language models for specific applications and ensuring their responsible deployment. Fine-tuning strategies, from simple head-level adjustments to sophisticated parameter-efficient methods like LoRa, empower developers to tailor general-purpose models to diverse and specialized tasks, unlocking immense potential across industries. The ability to adapt models efficiently, even with resource constraints through techniques like quantization, is critical for widespread AI adoption.

However, this power comes with the responsibility of understanding and mitigating risks. The phenomenon of jailbreaking, where models can be coaxed into generating harmful content despite safety measures, underscores the continuous challenge of AI alignment. Techniques like RLHF and PPO are at the forefront of addressing this, striving to instill models with human values of helpfulness and safety. As AI continues to evolve, the ongoing research and practical application of these fine-tuning and alignment strategies will be paramount in shaping a future where intelligent systems are not only capable but also trustworthy and beneficial to society.
