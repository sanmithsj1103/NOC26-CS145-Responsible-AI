# Research Overview: Representation Surgery

### Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have revolutionized how we interact with technology, but their immense power comes with challenges, including biases and uncontrolled generation. This lecture introduces "Representation Surgery," a novel approach to precisely control and de-bias these powerful models by manipulating their internal representations. At its heart, this work explores whether the contextual vectors produced by LLMs can be treated as well-behaved multivariate spaces, allowing for targeted interventions. The ability to steer these representations with minimal disruption to their core semantics offers a pathway to more ethical, reliable, and controllable AI systems, addressing critical concerns in responsible AI development.

The central question guiding this research is whether we can exploit the properties of these internal representations to achieve specific outcomes, such as removing unwanted biases or guiding text generation towards desired characteristics. This is not merely about filtering outputs but about fundamentally altering the underlying "thought process" of the model. By understanding and manipulating these high-dimensional spaces, Representation Surgery aims to provide a theoretical and practical framework for fine-grained control, ensuring that language models align more closely with human values and intentions without the prohibitive costs of traditional fine-tuning.

### Core Concepts
| Term | Definition | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`Representations`** | Contextual vectors generated by language models for sentences or tokens, encoding their meaning and relationships. | These are the fundamental units manipulated to control or de-bias language model behavior. |
| **`Guardedness`** | A state where a specific attribute (e.g., gender) cannot be classified from a language model's representations. | Ensures that sensitive information is not implicitly encoded or easily extractable, promoting fairness. |
| **`Affine Concept Erasure`** | An affine transformation (wx + b) applied to representations to make them guarded with respect to a target attribute. | A practical method for achieving guardedness by learning specific transformation parameters. |
| **`Steering Vectors`** | Vectors added to language model outputs to guide generation towards desired properties. | A common technique in literature, but this work provides theoretical justification and refinement. |
| **`Smallest Change Possible`** | The principle of modifying representations minimally to achieve a desired outcome, preserving unrelated semantics. | Crucial for maintaining the coherence and original meaning of the text while applying interventions. |
| **`First Moment Matching`** | An optimization objective to make the mean of two distributions (e.g., toxic and non-toxic representations) identical. | A foundational step in aligning distributions, providing a simple yet effective steering mechanism. |
| **`Second Moment Matching`** | An extension to first moment matching, aiming to align both the mean and covariance of two distributions. | Offers a more expressive and robust way to make distributions resemble each other, improving control. |
| **`TRP Gap`** | A metric used in de-biasing to measure the difference in classifier judgment across different categorical classes of a guarded attribute. | Quantifies the effectiveness of de-biasing by ensuring attribute independence in classification tasks. |
| **`Piecewise Affine`** | A function that applies different affine transformations based on certain conditions or input characteristics. | Allows for more nuanced and targeted interventions compared to a single global affine transformation. |

### Detailed Analysis
#### 1. The Challenge of Language Model Control and De-biasing
Language models (LMs) are trained to predict the next token, a process that inherently leads them to produce rich contextual representations—vectors that encapsulate the meaning of sentences. A core question in this research is whether these high-dimensional vectors can be treated as "well-behaved multivariate spaces" that can be exploited for specific purposes like de-biasing and controlling text generation. The goal is to manipulate these representations to make vectors from one distribution (e.g., toxic generations) resemble those from another (e.g., non-toxic generations).

A critical constraint for any such manipulation is the "smallest change possible" principle. This means that interventions should alter the representations just enough to achieve the desired effect (e.g., removing toxicity) while preserving all other semantics unrelated to the target attribute. For instance, if a sentence like "he asked John to off" is deemed toxic, changing it to "he asked John to immediately" should remove the toxicity without losing the core meaning of the request. This preservation of coherence is paramount for practical applications.

The concept of "guardedness" is introduced to formalize de-biasing. An attribute Z is guarded if it's impossible to train a classifier on the LM's representations to predict Z. For example, guarding gender means that a classifier should not be able to determine the gender of a noun based solely on its representation. "Affine Concept Erasure" is proposed as a method to achieve this: an affine transformation (Wx + B) is learned such that the transformed representations are guarded with respect to attribute Z.

#### 2. Context, Related Work, and Our Novel Approach
Existing literature in LM interpretability and controllability often employs several strategies. Fine-tuning is a common approach, but it's often not interpretable and can be computationally expensive. Another popular method involves "steering vectors," where a vector `v` is added to the LM's output to guide generation. A common heuristic for `v` is `mu_0 - mu_1`, where `mu_0` represents the average vector of a desirable class (e.g., non-toxic) and `mu_1` represents an undesirable class (e.g., toxic). While this often works empirically, it lacks strong theoretical justification.

De-biasing literature also exists, often focusing on making two classes indistinguishable. However, this typically doesn't care where the final representations land. For example, when guarding gender, the goal is merely to remove the difference between male and female representations. In contrast, for tasks like detoxification, there's an asymmetry: the goal is not just to remove the difference between toxic and non-toxic, but to ensure *all* representations land on the non-toxic side. This asymmetry is a key distinction that Representation Surgery addresses.

The proposed work provides a theoretical justification for steering vectors and extends their capabilities. It operates on the assumption that a classification dataset (sentences with labels, e.g., toxic/non-toxic) is available. This supervision allows the method to design interventions in a closed-form manner, making them efficient and interpretable.

#### 3. Contribution 1: Optimizing for First Moment Matching
The first contribution of Representation Surgery is framing the problem as a constrained optimization objective. The primary goal is to minimize the difference between the original vector and the intervened vector, adhering to the "smallest change possible" principle. The initial constraint is to make the mean of two distributions identical. Specifically, the objective is to find the smallest change such that the mean of the undesirable class (e.g., toxic) becomes equal to the mean of the desirable class (e.g., non-toxic).

The solution to this optimization problem, which involves finding a piecewise affine function, results in a simple yet powerful intervention. For vectors belonging to the undesirable class (let's say class `c`), the optimal intervention `W*` effectively becomes an identity transformation, and the bias `B` becomes `mu_c' - mu_c`. Here, `mu_c'` is the mean of the desirable class and `mu_c` is the mean of the undesirable class. This means that for undesirable vectors, the intervention shifts them by the difference between the desirable and undesirable means, effectively aligning their first moments. For vectors already in the desirable class, no change is applied. This provides a theoretical basis for the `mu_0 - mu_1` heuristic commonly used in steering vectors.

This intervention can be calculated easily at each inference step, requiring only the pre-computed means of the respective classes from the classification dataset.

#### 4. Contribution 2: Extending to First and Second Moment Matching
Building upon the first contribution, the research extends the optimization problem to match both the first and second moments (mean and covariance) of the distributions. This provides a more expressive steering function, allowing for a more complete transformation of the undesirable distribution to resemble the desirable one. The objective remains to make the smallest possible change to the original vector, but now subject to two constraints: matching the first moment and matching the second moment (covariance).

The solution to this extended optimization problem, while more complex mathematically, again yields a closed-form intervention. This solution is guaranteed to be the smallest change under this specific phrasing of the problem, ensuring that the transformed representations not only have the same mean but also the same spread and orientation as the target distribution. The lecture notes that this solution is related to optimal transport for Gaussian distributions, but the proof holds for arbitrary distributions as the claim is only to match the first and second moments. This more sophisticated intervention allows for a more robust and nuanced control over the representation space.

#### 5. Experimental Validation: De-biasing and Controlled Generation
The effectiveness of Representation Surgery is demonstrated through two main types of experiments:

* **De-biasing**: The method is applied to protect attributes without damaging the accuracy of other tasks. A popular metric for this is the "TRP gap" (True Positive Rate gap). This metric assesses whether a classifier's judgment for a multi-class problem (e.g., classifying professions from bios) is independent of a guarded attribute (e.g., gender). A small TRP gap indicates that the classifier does not rely on the guarded attribute. The experiments show that Representation Surgery significantly reduces the TRP gap, effectively removing gender bias, while maintaining high accuracy on the profession classification task. This demonstrates the ability to guard attributes without compromising the utility of the representations.

* **Controlling Generation**: The method is used to control the toxicity of generated sentences. The evaluation involves generating multiple sentences per prompt, using tools like Perspective API to measure toxicity, and then applying the intervention at every step of the generation process. A key advantage highlighted is that Representation Surgery offers a "no-gradient and cheap way" to control generation. Unlike other baselines that require expensive full parameter fine-tuning or extensive gradient calculations during inference, this method can be "dropped in" and provides strong control without significant computational overhead. This makes it highly practical for real-time applications where slow generation is unacceptable.

The overall insight is that global statistics of representations (means and covariances) can be effectively leveraged to control and de-bias language models. This is possible because attributes of interest are often encoded in an organized way within the LM's representation space.

### Key Takeaways
- [ ] Language models produce contextual representations that can be treated as well-behaved multivariate spaces.
- [ ] The goal of Representation Surgery is to de-bias and control generation by making minimal, targeted changes to these representations.
- [ ] "Guardedness" means an attribute cannot be classified from representations, and "Affine Concept Erasure" is a method to achieve it.
- [ ] The "smallest change possible" principle is crucial for preserving semantics unrelated to the target attribute during intervention.
- [ ] Existing steering vector heuristics (e.g., `mu_0 - mu_1`) are theoretically justified by optimizing for first moment matching.
- [ ] Matching both first and second moments (mean and covariance) provides a more expressive and robust steering function.
- [ ] The method requires a classification dataset for supervision but offers a no-gradient, cheap, and efficient way to intervene.
- [ ] Experimental results demonstrate effective de-biasing (reducing TRP gap) without accuracy loss and strong control over generation (e.g., toxicity reduction).
- [ ] Representation Surgery offers a practical alternative to expensive fine-tuning or gradient-heavy inference methods for LM control.

### Conclusion
Representation Surgery offers a compelling framework for enhancing the controllability and ethical alignment of large language models. By providing a theoretical foundation for manipulating internal representations and demonstrating practical, efficient interventions, this work moves beyond heuristic approaches to offer a principled way to steer model behavior. The ability to make the "smallest change possible" ensures that interventions are precise, preserving the rich semantics of the original representations while effectively mitigating biases or guiding generation towards desired outcomes.

The implications of this research are significant for the development of responsible AI. It provides a powerful tool for developers to de-bias models against sensitive attributes like gender, ensuring fairness in applications. Furthermore, its capacity for cheap, no-gradient control over generation opens doors for real-time content moderation, creative text generation, and personalized AI interactions that adhere to specific guidelines. As language models become increasingly integrated into society, methods like Representation Surgery will be indispensable for building AI systems that are not only powerful but also trustworthy, transparent, and aligned with human values.
